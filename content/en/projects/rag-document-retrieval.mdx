This was a four-day competition to retrieve the documents relevant to either a standalone
question or a multi-turn conversation and submit the top three results. The metric was mean
average precision (MAP). In our
first meeting, we split the work into two approaches—LLM-based retrieval and embedding
retrieval—then ran them in parallel and shared the findings. On the two-person team, I handled data
augmentation and embedding retrieval.

## Two retrieval paths from the first meeting

The team's LLM-based path extracted keywords from each question, retrieved candidates with
TF-IDF, and asked an LLM to choose the final three documents. It worked well for some questions,
but its results varied substantially.

For the embedding path, I compared six models.

- KR-SBERT
- KoSimCSE
- klue/bert-base
- BAAI/bge-large-en-v1.5
- OpenAI text-embedding-3-small
- Upstage embedding-query/passage

Upstage embedding-query/passage performed best, with a MAP of 0.8848.

## Narrowing the search from models to document representation

After selecting the embedding model, I found that combining sparse and dense retrieval improved
the score substantially over either method alone. Within hybrid retrieval, I compared the order
of operations, score-combination weights, cosine and Euclidean distance, and HNSW index search.
Changes to these details produced only small differences, so the next experiments changed the
text being retrieved instead.

Simple question augmentation lowered the score. Rewriting the questions with an LLM produced a
small improvement, which led to the hypothesis that rewriting documents into a representation
better suited to embedding retrieval might also help. Restructuring document content for
retrieval produced the largest gain. In the final approach, I combined candidates from the
original text, rewritten questions, and rewritten documents.

## The result after four days

The team was originally formed with four members, but two withdrew, leaving the two of us to
run the actual competition. With only four days to test the LLM and embedding approaches in
parallel, we did not train and apply a separate reranker. Starting from the embedding-model
comparison, we iteratively changed retrieval order, similarity measures, and finally the
representation of both questions and documents. We placed second overall.
